Title: Italian Grand Prix 2026 -- Circuit Notes and Team Suitability, Autodromo Nazionale Monza
Source: Aggregated from f1chronicle.substack.com, the-race.com, scuderiafans.com, blog.f1livepulse.com
Date retrieved: 2026-09-05
Race date: September 6, 2026 (Round 13 of 23)

CIRCUIT CHARACTERISTICS
Monza is normally run in the lowest-downforce trim of the season to protect top speed through its long
full-throttle sections. Braking stability and traction on exit through the First Chicane and the Ascari
chicane are the two factors most likely to separate the front of the field -- pure straight-line speed
matters more here than anywhere else on the calendar, but a car that can't put power down cleanly out of
the chicanes will lose it all back under braking and acceleration.

2026 POWER UNIT CONTEXT
The 2026 power units deliver a much greater share of power from the electrical/hybrid system than in
previous years. This puts extra emphasis on energy management and deployment across Monza's long
straights, and Pirelli has flagged that the rear axle will face greater traction demands than usual as
drivers get back to full power out of the chicanes -- rear tire management is a bigger factor this year
than it has historically been at Monza.

TEAM SUITABILITY FOR THIS RACE
- Mercedes is the early favorite for the Italian Grand Prix: the W17 is expected to suit Monza's demands
  particularly well, consistent with Mercedes' dominant form across the 2026 season so far.
- Ferrari is running its new, more powerful and efficient ADUO-2 power unit and has a strong motivation
  to perform well at its home race -- this is Ferrari's most important race of the year for team morale
  and sponsor visibility, in addition to championship points.
- McLaren arrives off the back of two consecutive victories (see 05_recent_form_and_last_race.txt),
  giving Norris and Piastri real momentum heading into a track that rewards a well-sorted low-drag setup.
- Red Bull is considered a potential dark horse: the RB22 may suit Monza's characteristics more naturally
  than it has suited recent circuits.

FANTASY IMPLICATIONS
Monza tends to produce closer, more overtaking-heavy racing than most circuits on the calendar (slipstream
effects on the long straights make passing easier), which historically inflates the "position gained" and
"overtake" bonus points in F1 Fantasy scoring relative to a typical race. A driver starting mid-grid on a
car with straight-line pace could plausibly out-score a pole-sitter on overtake and position-gained bonuses
alone.
