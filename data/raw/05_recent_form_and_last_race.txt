LATEST RACE: ITALIAN GRAND PRIX, MONZA (Round 13)
Result order: 1st Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes), 2nd George Russell (Mercedes), 3rd Max Verstappen (Red Bull),
4th Lando Norris (McLaren), 5th Oscar Piastri (McLaren), 6th Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari).
Charles Leclerc (Ferrari) retired with a crash at the Parabolica on lap 2 -- a DNF. Lance Stroll and
Fernando Alonso (both Aston Martin) also failed to finish. Sergio Perez received a 5-second time penalty
applied after the race, which did not change his finishing position outside the points.

STANDOUT STORYLINE: Antonelli started Monza from P19 after a 30-place grid penalty, then produced 26
overtakes over the race distance and set the fastest lap on his way to victory -- one of the most dominant
recovery drives of the season. Russell followed home in 2nd for a Mercedes 1-2. This is the single biggest
data point behind the "penalty-recalculation" and "hot-streak momentum vs. car pace" learnings in
learnings/learnings.json -- see entries #2 and #3, which were derived directly from scoring this result
against the pre-race prediction.

FORM HEADING INTO THE SPANISH GRAND PRIX (Round 14, Madrid -- first-ever F1 race at this circuit)
- Kimi Antonelli is now the clear form driver as well as championship leader: a Monza win from P19 extends
  his points lead over Russell to 66 (see 04_championship_standings.txt), and Mercedes has now won two of
  the last two races (Zandvoort via Norris was the exception before that) with Antonelli/Russell finishing
  1-2 at Monza specifically.
- Max Verstappen's Monza podium (P3) is his best result in several rounds -- worth watching as a possible
  value pick if Red Bull's pace is genuinely trending up rather than a Monza-specific power/slipstream
  effect (Madrid's mixed street/permanent layout is a very different test, see 06_madrid_circuit_notes.txt).
- Charles Leclerc's Monza DNF (crash, lap 2) is a blemish on an otherwise-solid recent Ferrari run and
  should weigh against picking him as a Captain/DRS Boost target until his form re-stabilizes, even though
  Ferrari's season-long standings position (3rd in constructors') is still strong.
- Isack Hadjar remains out of the car with a wrist injury (missed Zandvoort and Monza, his third
  consecutive absence will be Madrid); Liam Lawson continues to deputize at Red Bull and Yuki Tsunoda
  continues at Racing Bulls -- this seat arrangement is already reflected in 02_driver_prices.txt. Red Bull
  has stated it hopes to have Hadjar back for the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in Baku, i.e. not this race.
- 2026's new power unit regulations (greater electrical/hybrid contribution) and active-aero rules continue
  to keep the competitive order fluid race to race -- this is why current-season form matters more than
  historical reputation or a single result when picking a fantasy team this year.

IMPLICATIONS FOR FANTASY PICKS
A driver's raw championship position and their last-race result can disagree (e.g. Verstappen is not
championship-competitive this season but just produced his best result in rounds with a Monza podium).
Both season-long standings (04_championship_standings.txt) and recent-race form matter for choosing a
captain / DRS Boost target, and they should be weighed together with circuit-specific suitability
(06_madrid_circuit_notes.txt) -- especially this week, since Madrid is a brand-new circuit with zero
historical data, so current form and car characteristics matter more than usual relative to track-specific
history.
